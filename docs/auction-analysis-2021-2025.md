# MLFF Auction Analysis, 2021–2025

Five years of auction spending set against what those players actually scored and
what the teams that bought them actually did.

Generated by `scripts/auction-analysis.js`; every table below is reproduced as a
CSV in [`data/analysis/`](../data/analysis).

---

## What the data supports (and what it doesn't)

| | |
|---|---|
| **League format** | 10 teams, $300 auction budget, 16 roster spots |
| **Starting lineup** | 1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, 1 K, 1 DST (9 starters, 7 bench) |
| **Salary data** | Complete, all five auctions (2021–2025) |
| **Player fantasy points** | **2021–2023 only** — the CBS scrape never captured FPTS for the 2024 and 2025 auctions |
| **Team points** | Complete, all five years (standings + weekly results) |

So: **spend-by-team-by-position runs the full five years. Anything that ties dollars
to player points is a three-year sample (480 roster spots).** Back-filling 2024–2025
is the single highest-value data fix before the 2026 auction — see
[Back-filling 2024–2025](#back-filling-20242025-points) below. `auction-analysis.js`
detects which seasons carry points automatically, so once the CSVs have them, a
re-run widens every table here with no code change.

Part of the gap was self-inflicted: the scraper contained
`if (year < 2021 || year === 2024) { TotalFPTS = null }`, which discarded 2024's
points regardless of what CBS returned. That is fixed. 2025 was scraped pre-season,
before any points existed.

Two more notes on the source data:

- The `POS` column means "draft slot" in 2021–2023 (`RES` = bench) and "actual
  position" in 2024–2025. Only `ELIG` is consistent, so all position splits use it.
- `Total FPTS` is the player's full-season total. `Active FPTS` is what he scored
  while in the drafting team's starting lineup — the points the buyer actually
  banked. The gap between them is the cost of bench spots, byes, injuries and
  bad start/sit calls.

## 1. Where the money goes

League-wide share of the $3,000 in the room, by position:

| Pos | 5-yr avg share | $ per team | 2021 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| RB | **41.5%** | $124 | 45.7 → 42.0 → 36.6 → 40.1 → 43.1 |
| WR | **39.1%** | $117 | 35.9 → 41.4 → 41.5 → 39.2 → 37.4 |
| QB | 12.0% | $36 | 10.3 → 9.6 → 12.7 → 13.2 → **14.0** |
| TE | 6.3% | $19 | 7.0 → 6.0 → 7.7 → 6.4 → **4.5** |
| K | 0.6% | $2 | flat |
| DST | 0.5% | $1.50 | flat |

**80.6% of every dollar in this league goes to RB and WR.** That has been true every
year within a few points. The only real market movement in five years is QB drifting
up (+3.7 points of cap share, average price $16 → $20) and TE collapsing (average
price $16.60 in 2023 → $8.90 in 2025).

Full table: [`league_spend_by_position.csv`](../data/analysis/league_spend_by_position.csv)

## 3. Allocation barely predicts anything. Buying well does.

Correlation across 50 team-seasons (2021–2025), z-scored within each year so
league-wide scoring drift doesn't contaminate it:

| Spend variable | vs Points For | vs Wins |
|---|---|---|
| RB spend | −0.09 | +0.20 |
| WR spend | −0.04 | **−0.36** |
| QB spend | +0.19 | +0.22 |
| TE spend | +0.05 | +0.09 |
| DST spend | +0.37 | +0.19 |
| Top-3 buys, combined | −0.13 | −0.02 |
| Total spent on $50+ players | −0.15 | −0.02 |
| Number of $10+ players | +0.07 | −0.11 |

Nothing here is strong. **How you split the $300 across positions explains almost
none of the variance in scoring or wins.** (The DST number is noise — the entire
variable ranges from $1 to $3.)

Now the same exercise for *quality* of buying, over the 30 team-seasons with player
points:

| Metric | vs Points For | vs Wins |
|---|---|---|
| Drafted **Total** FPTS per dollar | **+0.53** | **+0.42** |
| Drafted **Active** FPTS per dollar | +0.39 | +0.32 |
| % of team points that came from drafted players | +0.06 | +0.08 |

That is the whole story in two tables. The auction is not won by choosing the right
budget shape. It is won by paying less than market for points — and the shape you
end up with is mostly a by-product.

Full tables: [`allocation_vs_results_correlation.csv`](../data/analysis/allocation_vs_results_correlation.csv),
[`draft_efficiency_vs_results.csv`](../data/analysis/draft_efficiency_vs_results.csv)

## 4. The marginal-dollar table

This is the most directly usable output. For each position and price band it asks:
*compared with just taking a $1–2 player at this position, how many extra points did
the extra dollars buy?* 2021–2023, 480 roster spots.

| Pos | Price band | n | Avg price | Avg pts | Extra pts | Extra $ | **Pts per extra $** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K | $3–9 | 5 | $4 | 136 | +12 | $3 | 4.47 |
| **QB** | **$10–24** | 11 | $19 | **229** | **+72** | **$17** | **4.13** |
| WR | $3–9 | 48 | $5 | 52 | +14 | $4 | 3.51 |
| RB | $3–9 | 33 | $6 | 61 | +15 | $4 | 3.47 |
| TE | $3–9 | 10 | $6 | 54 | +13 | $4 | 3.13 |
| **QB** | **$50–69** | 5 | $58 | **280** | **+123** | $57 | **2.18** |
| WR | $10–24 | 39 | $16 | 67 | +29 | $15 | 1.96 |
| TE | $50–69 | 3 | $57 | 113 | +72 | $55 | 1.30 |
| WR | $25–49 | 27 | $33 | 77 | +39 | $32 | 1.23 |
| WR | $50–69 | 18 | $56 | 100 | +63 | $55 | 1.14 |
| RB | $25–49 | 22 | $36 | 83 | +37 | $35 | 1.05 |
| QB | $25–49 | 9 | $36 | 191 | +35 | $34 | 1.00 |
| WR | $70+ | 9 | $79 | 108 | +71 | $77 | 0.92 |
| RB | $50–69 | 14 | $58 | 97 | +51 | $57 | 0.89 |
| TE | $10–24 | 8 | $15 | 53 | +12 | $14 | 0.88 |
| RB | $70+ | 18 | $83 | 115 | +69 | $81 | 0.84 |
| RB | $10–24 | 24 | $16 | 55 | +9 | $15 | 0.60 |
| **TE** | **$25–49** | 5 | $34 | **42** | **+2** | $33 | **0.06** |

Three things jump out:

1. **QB is the standing inefficiency.** A $10–24 QB returned 229 points — more than
   double any RB or WR tier at any price — while the room spent 12% of the cap on the
   position. Even the $50–69 QBs (280 points) beat every non-QB tier on marginal
   value. The room has started to notice (QB share up every year since 2022), which
   is exactly why it's worth knowing whether it's still mispriced in 2026.
2. **The $3–9 band is the best value at every position.** 3.1–3.5 points per marginal
   dollar across RB, WR and TE, versus 0.6–1.2 in the $10–49 range. Mid-priced players
   are where money goes to die.
3. **TE at $25–49 is the single worst allocation in the league.** Five buys, average
   $34, average 42 points — *below* the 40.5 points a $1–2 tight end returned. Either
   pay $50+ for a genuinely elite TE (113 points) or spend $1. Never $30.

Full tables: [`marginal_value_over_min_bid.csv`](../data/analysis/marginal_value_over_min_bid.csv),
[`price_tier_return.csv`](../data/analysis/price_tier_return.csv)

## 5. Big money is bad money

Grouping every auction by spend rank:

| Rank group | Avg price | Avg pts | Pts per $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| #1–10 most expensive | $80 | 120 | 1.51 |
| #11–20 | $59 | 130 | 2.21 |
| #21–30 | $45 | 104 | 2.30 |
| #31–50 | $28 | 91 | 3.26 |
| #51–100 | $10 | 73 | 7.52 |
| #101–160 | $1.50 | 78 | 52.7 |

*(three-year averages)*

The ten most expensive players in each auction — $800 of the $3,000 in the room,
27% of all league money — returned the worst rate in the draft. And the hit rates
in section 4's underlying data are brutal:

- RB at $50–69: **7%** hit rate. RB at $70+: **11%**.
- WR at $50+: **0%**. Twenty-seven wide receivers bought for $50 or more across three
  years, *not one* returned the position's average points per dollar.

The worst buys are almost all expensive running backs — Nick Chubb ($84 → 7 points),
J.K. Dobbins ($39 → 0), Christian McCaffrey ($101 → 37), Jonathan Taylor ($98 → 59),
Dalvin Cook ($99 → 84), Austin Ekeler ($81 → 65). The best buys are almost all $1
quarterbacks: Stafford ($1 → 285), Prescott ($1 → 272), Goff ($1 → 252), Lawrence
($1 → 243), Hurts ($1 → 202).

The behavioural split across five years is stark. Counting big-ticket buys per
franchise:

| Team | $70+ buys | $50+ buys | Avg PF |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Generals** | **2** | **13** | 1083 |
| Munich Cowboys | 6 | 13 | 1050 |
| Masters of Disaster | **7** | 10 | 975 |
| Homers Heroes | 7 | 12 | 976 |
| Relegators *(2 yrs)* | 4 | 4 | 859 |

The Generals are the most aggressive team in the league at $50–69 — 13 buys, more
than anyone — and the *least* aggressive above $70, with two in five years. That is
the exact shape the marginal-value table rewards, and they have the best record and
the highest scoring in the window.

Full tables: [`spend_rank_return.csv`](../data/analysis/spend_rank_return.csv),
[`best_buys.csv`](../data/analysis/best_buys.csv),
[`worst_buys.csv`](../data/analysis/worst_buys.csv)

## 6. Kickers and defenses score more than you think

An accident of the scoring system, but a real one:

| Pos | Avg season points, drafted players (2021–23) |
|---|---|
| QB | 186 |
| **K** | **125** |
| **DST** | **91** |
| RB | 71 |
| WR | 64 |
| TE | 52 |

The average drafted kicker outscored the average drafted RB by 54 points a season,
for $2. This does *not* mean spend on kickers — the spread between the best and worst
drafted kicker is small enough that a $5 kicker beat a $1 kicker by 12 points. It
means the position is worth actually managing during the season rather than treating
as an afterthought, and it means a bye-week hole at K or DST costs more than a bye
at WR.

Full table: [`position_return_on_spend.csv`](../data/analysis/position_return_on_spend.csv)

## 7. The draft is only two-thirds of the season

Points scored by drafted players while in the drafting team's lineup, as a share of
that team's total scoring:

| | |
|---|---|
| League average | **70%** |
| Best team-season | LA Losers 2023, 96% |
| Worst team-season | Homers Heroes 2023, 39% |
| Team-seasons under 60% | 8 of 30 |

Roughly **30% of a team's points come from players who weren't bought at the
auction** — waivers, free agents and trades. And critically, the share of points
coming from your draft has *no* relationship to winning (r = +0.08). Teams that
hit big on waivers (Munich Cowboys: 62% from draft, 10.7 wins a year) do just as
well as teams that live off their draft.

Practical implication: the auction is worth roughly two-thirds of the season's
scoring, and the last few dollars are better spent on lottery tickets you'll churn
than on the third-most-expensive player in a tier.

Full table: [`team_draft_capture.csv`](../data/analysis/team_draft_capture.csv)

## 8. Masters of Disaster, specifically

| Year | RB | WR | QB | TE | PF | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $94 | $93 | $37 | $68 | 987 | 9–8 |
| 2022 | $86 | **$180** | $18 | $12 | 1047 | 6–11 |
| 2023 | $27 | **$182** | $4 | $84 | 890 | 5–11 |
| 2024 | $86 | **$149** | $30 | $29 | 947 | 5–11 |
| 2025 | $147 | $46 | **$65** | $40 | 1003 | **11–5** |

Three straight years (2022–24) of pouring 50–61% of the cap into wide receivers
produced 16 wins in 48 games. The receivers were bought in the band with a 0% hit
rate: Cooper Kupp at $77 (81 points), Ja'Marr Chase at $75 (120) and $81 (93),
Justin Jefferson at $84 (76).

Two patterns stand out across the five years:

- **Most $70+ buys in the league (7).** Only 10 of MoD's buys were $50 or more, so
  seven of ten big swings landed in the worst-value band on the board. The Generals,
  by contrast, made 13 buys at $50+ and only 2 at $70+.
- **Quarterback has tracked the record almost exactly.** $4 total on QB in 2023 →
  5–11. $18 in 2022 → 6–11. $65 in 2025 (Jayden Daniels at $57) → 11–5. The 2021
  season, the other one with real QB money ($37), was the only other winning year.

2025 was a near-total inversion — WR spend cut from $149 to $46, RB up to $147, real
money at QB — and it produced the franchise's best season in the window: 11–5, 1003
points, third in the league in scoring. One season is one season, but it moved in the
direction every other table here points.

The tight end spending is more defensible than the 15.5%-of-cap headline suggests:
it's been barbelled, not stuck in the dead zone. Kelce at $68 in 2021 returned 113
points (the good $50–69 band); Kelce at $80 in 2023 returned 95 (thin value); 2022
was $9 and $3. The one buy squarely in the worst band on the whole board was Mark
Andrews at $29 in 2024, and George Kittle at $39 in 2025 sits in the same dead zone.

## Carrying into the 2026 auction

1. **Do not build a budget shape. Build a price list.** Position allocation
   correlates ~0 with results; points-per-dollar correlates +0.53. Decide what each
   player is worth in points and buy whoever the room misprices.
2. **Be the last team to spend at RB/WR above $50.** Combined hit rate on $50+ WRs
   is 0% and on $50+ RBs is ~9%. That money is 27% of the room and returns the worst
   rate in the auction.
3. **Buy a quarterback in the $10–25 range, or wait for a $1 one.** Best marginal
   value on the board at 4.13 points per extra dollar, and the $1–2 QB pool still
   averaged 157 points. The one band to avoid is $3–9, which underperformed the
   $1 QBs outright.
4. **Tight end is binary.** $50+ or $1–2. The $25–49 band returned 0.06 points per
   marginal dollar — statistically indistinguishable from lighting $33 on fire.
5. **Load up in the $3–9 band.** Best marginal value at RB, WR and TE. With seven
   bench spots and ~30% of scoring coming from in-season pickups, volume of cheap
   upside beats one more mid-priced starter.
6. **Fix the data before draft day.** Re-run the CBS scrape for 2024 and 2025 player
   points. Two more seasons would nearly double the sample and, more importantly,
   tell us whether the QB inefficiency has already been arbitraged away — QB share
   of cap has risen every year since 2022.

## Back-filling 2024–2025 points

Two routes. Both end at the same place — populated `Total FPTS` / `Active FPTS`
columns in `data/yearly-stats/draft_results_2024.csv` and `_2025.csv`.

**Route A — scrape it.** Needs CBS credentials and a browser that can reach the
internet.

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm scrape-draft-results 2024 2025
```

Credentials come from `CBS_SPORTS_EMAIL`/`CBS_SPORTS_PASSWORD` or `cbslogin`/`cbspw`,
in the environment or `.env.local`. The login field is CBS's **email address**, not
the account's username.

Runs headed and interactive at a desktop (so you can clear a captcha), and headless
when no terminal is attached. A failed login writes a screenshot and the page HTML to
`data/scrape-debug/` (gitignored).

Two things that cost time the first time round:

- **Managed environments inject variables only at session start.** Editing them
  mid-session changes nothing for the session already running — it keeps the values
  it booted with. Start a fresh session after changing a credential.
- **Chromium ignores `NODE_EXTRA_CA_CERTS` and the system trust store.** Behind a
  TLS-inspecting egress gateway it must be launched direct rather than through
  `HTTPS_PROXY`, or every request fails with `ERR_CONNECTION_RESET`. The scraper
  detects this, pins the gateway CA's public key and handles it; no flags needed.

**Route B — save the pages.** No credentials handled by any script; works when
browser automation is blocked.

1. Open each page in a browser you're already logged into:
   - `https://mlffatl.football.cbssports.com/draft/results/2024:Pre-season:MLFF%20AUCTION3/`
   - `https://mlffatl.football.cbssports.com/draft/results/2025:Pre-season:Pre-season/`
2. Save as `data/raw/draft_2024.html` and `data/raw/draft_2025.html`.
3. `pnpm parse-draft-results-html`

Either way, finish with `pnpm auction-analysis`. Both scripts report how many rows
came back carrying points, so a silent empty result isn't possible.
